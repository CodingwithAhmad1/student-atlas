Title: Salary requirements to nominate a worker
Description: Information on salary and employment condition requirements if you wish to nominate workers for subclass 482, 186 and 187.
Alert: Skilled visa income thresholds
Skilled visa income thresholds are increasing in line with changes to annual Average Weekly Ordinary Time Earnings. For more information see Annual indexation of skilled visa income thresholds from 1 July 2026.
Employers who wish to nominate workers for subclass 482, 494, 186 and 187 must meet certain requirements. If you will pay the overseas worker an annual salary less than AUD250,000 you must show that: you have determined the annual market salary rate (AMSR) correctly and you will not pay the overseas worker less than the AMSR, that is, less than an Australian worker would be paid and both the AMSR and the overseas worker’s pay, excluding non-monetary benefits, is no less than the relevant income threshold. These requirements will help to ensure that overseas workers are not paid less than an Australian worker doing the same work. They will also stop these visa programs being used to undercut the Australian labour market. If you intend to pay the nominated overseas worker less than the equivalent Australian worker, we will refuse the nomination.
Core Skills Income Threshold
Skills in Demand visa (SID) (subclass 482) applications in the Core Skills stream must meet the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT). The CSIT also applies to Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) (subclass 186) nominations lodged on or after 7 December 2024. The CSIT for ENS and SID Core Skills Stream nomination applications is as follows: AUD79,423 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027. AUD76,515 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026. AUD73,150 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 7 December 2024 and 30 June 2025.
Specialist Skills Income Threshold
Skills in Demand visa (SID) (subclass 482) applications in the Specialist Skills stream must meet the Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT). The SSIT for SID Specialist Skills Stream nomination applications is as follows: AUD146,576 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027. AUD141,210 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026. AUD135,000 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 7 December 2024 and 30 June 2025.
Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold
The Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) is used to assess the Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 494) and the Regional Sponsored Migration Scheme (RSMS) (subclass 187). The TSMIT for Nomination applications is as follows: AUD79,423 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027. AUD76,515 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026. AUD73,150 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2024 and 30 June 2025. AUD70,000 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2023 and 30 June 2024. AUD53,900 for Nomination applications lodged on and between 1 July 2013 and 30 June 2023. Both the AMSR for the nominated occupation and the workers guaranteed annual earnings must be at least as much as the relevant TSMIT at the time of lodgement. The TSMIT does not include non-monetary benefits such as accommodation or a car. Employers must pay such benefits in addition to the TSMIT.
Indexation of salary thresholds
Income thresholds are indexed annually to ensure fair remuneration for overseas skilled workers. Salary indexation changes apply from 1 July each year. If you lodge a sponsorship or nomination application on or after this date, you must meet the relevant income threshold for that year.
Annual market salary rate
The Annual market salary rate (AMSR) is the earnings an Australian citizen or Australian permanent resident earns or would earn: for equivalent full‑time work for one year at the same workplace and location. Determining the AMSR The method of determining AMSR will depend on whether you have an equivalent Australian worker who is: employed in the same workplace as the nominee; and at the same location as the nominee; and performing equivalent work to the nominee; An Australian worker who is more or less experienced than the nominee and does similar work at a different pay grade is not an equivalent worker to the nominee. The Table below shows how you can determine the AMSR. You may need to provide: the applicable Fair Work instrument, state industrial instrument or transitional instrument that applies. This includes the relevant salary level or classification that applies relevant employment documents, which may include payslips or employment contracts for an equivalent Australian worker relevant information, which may include: jobs and skills Australia occupation and industry profiles job advertisements from a national recruitment website or national print media that are in English and specify the salary arrangements for the advertised position remuneration surveys completed by a reputable organisation. If you provide only generic market salary data or surveys, we may refuse the nomination written advice from unions or employer associations. How to determine the AMSR Where there is an equivalent Australian worker Where there is not an equivalent Australian worker Where there is a Fair Work instrument, state industrial instrument or transitional instrument (an industrial instrument) AMSR will be determined by the industrial instrument OR by relevant employment documents, provided the amount in the employment documents is not less than the amount in the industrial instrument. AMSR will be determined by the industrial instrument OR by relevant information, provided the amount in that information is not less than the amount in the industrial instrument. Where there is not a Fair Work instrument, state industrial instrument or transitional instrument AMSR will be determined by the relevant employment documents. AMSR will be determined by the relevant information. For more information on relevant instruments, visit the Fair Work Commission.
