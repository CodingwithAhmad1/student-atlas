FOREWORD
The Malawi Department of Immigration and Citizenship Services is pleased to
publish the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for Dual Citizenship which
will act as a reference document in the operationalization of the dual
citizenship law thereby ensuring efficient and effective service delivery.
The SOPs have been developed to provide direction and good practice in the
operationalization of the dual citizenship law. It will act as a guide in terms
of operations and decision making by all Immigration Officers in the provision
of dual citizenship services.
The SOPs are intended to provide practical guidance to the Immigration
officers in the course of providing dual citizenship services. It will provide an
easy reference to all Immigration Officers to consult when in need of support
in carrying out official duties of the dual citizenship services.
Any changes to the SOPs will be authorized by the Director General of the
Department of Immigration and Citizenship Services, and advised by an
amendment circular.
The SOPs come into operation once approved by the Director General of the
Department of Immigration and Citizenship Services.
Hannings Mlotha

BACKGROUND
Citizenship issues in Malawi are governed by the 1966 Citizenship Act,
Chapter 15:01 of the Laws of Malawi. The 1966 law permitted Malawians
under the age of 22 years to maintain dual citizenship, but it limited persons
of full age and capacity to be dual citizens.
On 12th December, 2018, the Malawi National Assembly passed the Malawi
Citizenship Amendment Bill to amend the 1966 Citizenship Act, allowing
persons of full age and capacity to hold citizenship of another country in
addition to their Malawi citizenship.
The dual citizenship bill was assented into law in March 2019 and
operationalization of dual citizenship regulations were gazetted in November
2021 and announced to the general public through a Government Notice
Number 59 of the Malawi Citizenship Act.
In Malawi, dual citizenship is the status whereby a person is concurrently
regarded as a citizen of two countries. Only Malawians by birth or descent
are eligible to acquire dual citizenship. Persons who were naturalized,
registered or conferred the citizenship of Malawi are NOT eligible for the
Malawi dual citizenship.
TYPES OF DUAL CITIZENSHIP APPLICATIONS
There are three circumstances that qualifies citizens of Malawi by birth and
descent to submit an application for the Malawi dual citizenship:

1. Notification and Registration of dual citizenship
A person of full age who to his/her knowledge has obtained the
citizenship of another country, is required to submit an application of
notice and registration for the acquisition of a Malawi dual citizenship.
A notification and registration application has to be submitted in Form
AA as set out in the First Schedule. (see appendix 1)
2. Restoration of Malawi Citizenship
This is an application of a person of full age who lost the Malawi
citizenship through the acquisition of another country’s citizenship due
to the old Malawi citizenship law that prohibited dual citizenship of
persons of full age.
An application for the restoration of Malawi citizenship shall be
submitted in Form AC as set out in the First Schedule. (see appendix 2)
3. Notification of dual citizenship upon attaining the age of 19 years
An application for registration as a dual citizen of full age for a minor
who has attained the age of 19 years, and has realized that he/she is a
dual citizen, shall be submitted in Form AE as set out in the First
Schedule. (see appendix 3)

STEP BY STEP PROCEDURE FOR DUAL CITIZENSHIP APPLICATION
PROCESS
Step One: Collection and completion of Dual Citizenship Application Forms
 Malawi citizens by birth or descent who want to acquire dual citizenship
must first fill in an applicable application form which can only be
obtained online on the following website www.immigration.gov.mw.
 Applicants are required to provide all the necessary information
supporting the application.
 Applications are processed in Blantyre, Lilongwe, Mangochi, Mzuzu,
and Malawi Embassies/Consular offices across the globe with an
Immigration Attaché.
Step Two: Documents Evaluation
 Applicants must submit their completed dual citizenship application
forms together with all the required supporting documents as set out in
the checklists.
 An Immigration Officer is required to evaluate and verify the
authenticity of all the support documents accompanying the application
forms and advise on any additional attachments as per requirements.
 All photocopies must be duly certified as true copies of the original by
a Commissioner for Oath.
 The application is then registered in the dual citizenship
functionality/ledger and endorsed ‘the next level’.
Page 7 of 8
Step Three: Payment
 Applications that meet all the requirements and that have been fully
endorsed are subjected to upfront payments through the designated
Bank.
 Every applicant gets receipt(s) as proof of payment.
Step Four: Application Processing
 Once payments have been completed, the application (in case the
submission was made at any Immigration Office captured in step one
(1) other than the Headquarters) is sent to the Immigration
Headquarters.
 The Citizenship Registry opens a file and a file number is given and
name is endorsed in the open sheet.
 Application files are sent to the Minister of Homeland Security with
recommendations by the Director General or his/her delegates.
 When the application has been approved or rejected by the Minister, it
is sent back to the Immigration Headquarters.
 In case of rejection or approval, applicants are officially informed by
the Citizenship office that received that application.
CLEARANCE OF DUAL CITIZENS ON ENTRY
 Dual citizens are not subjected to visa entry requirements even if the
other nationality requires entry visa into Malawi. They will be treated
as Returning Residents.

 As per the 1964 Immigration Act, a dual citizen arriving at the border
or airport within Malawi, has to present him/herself at the nearest
Immigration Officer.
 The dual citizen has to declare to an Immigration Officer that he is a
dual citizen.
 The immigration officer should demand the two passports and indicate
on the schedule passport numbers of the two countries.
 If the Malawi passport is not valid, an Immigration officer should
interview the person in order to gather further evidence of citizenship.
 After interview and establishment that the person is a Malawian, he/she
is allowed entry.
 If not, further investigations are carried out to determine how the
Malawi passport was acquired and the person could be refused entry
and confiscation of the Malawi passport.
