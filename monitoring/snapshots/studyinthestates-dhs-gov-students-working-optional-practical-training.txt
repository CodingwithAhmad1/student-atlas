Training Opportunities in the United States
The United States allows eligible F and M students the opportunity to gain on-the-job learning that supplements knowledge gained in their academic studies, known as practical training. To participate in practical training as an F or M student, you do not need to change your nonimmigrant status. Instead, you must work with your designated school official (DSO) to ensure your eligibility as well as apply for and receive proper authorization before starting any practical training.
Remember: You must have proper authorization before beginning any practical training. Learn more on the Applying for Practical Training page. Additionally, remember that F and M students who receive financial compensation of any kind must apply for a Social Security number.
Curricular Practical Training for F Students
Curricular practical training (CPT) is an alternative work/study, internship, cooperative education or other type of required practicum that a sponsoring employer offers through cooperative agreements with your college or university. CPT is only available for F students when it is an integral part of an established curriculum. Unlike pre-completion optional practical training (OPT), CPT can be full time and is not restricted by a weekly 20-hour work limit.
To qualify for CPT, you must have completed one full academic year of study at an SEVP-certified school, unless you are a graduate student whose program requires immediate CPT. Students who complete one year or more of full-time CPT are ineligible to participate in post-completion OPT.
To receive permission to begin CPT, you must make an official request for CPT authorization to your DSO so that the school can confirm that you are participating in an established curriculum where practical training is integral and make a determination that the training is directly related to your major area of study. If eligible, your DSO will authorize you for CPT in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS) and provide you with an updated Form I-20, “Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status,” endorsed for CPT.
If you are interested in participating in CPT, talk to your DSO to ensure you understand federal regulations and your school’s specific CPT policy. Your DSO is the best person to work with to ensure that you follow both federal regulations and your school’s policy. See the August 2026 Broadcast Message: Guidance for Designated School Officials regarding Curricular Practical Training for more information.
Optional Practical Training for F Students
OPT is a form of practical training, often paid, that directly relates to your program of study as an F student. Students enrolled in English language training programs are not eligible for OPT.
You can apply for OPT during your program of study, known as pre-completion OPT, or after you finish your program of study, known as post-completion OPT. Pre-completion OPT is limited to 20 hours per week while school is in session but can be full time during breaks and annual vacation.
To apply for OPT, first request approval from your DSO. Your DSO will confirm your eligibility and, if eligible, will recommend you for OPT in SEVIS and endorse your Form I-20 for OPT.
You must then file the Form I-765, “Application for Employment Authorization,” and supporting documents with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). If approved, USCIS will issue you an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). You cannot begin OPT until you receive authorization from USCIS.
You can apply for 12 months of OPT at each education level (e.g., 12 months of OPT at the bachelor’s level and another 12 months of OPT at the master’s level). If you are an F student earning a degree in a science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) field, you may be eligible for the STEM OPT extension. For more information, visit the STEM OPT page on Study in the States.
F Students and Entrepreneurship
The U.S. government considers starting your own business in the United States, also known as entrepreneurship, as work. Therefore, if you are an F student who plans to start your own business in the United States, you must qualify and apply for OPT.
OPT, and thus your business, must relate to your program of study and can occur either before (pre-completion OPT) or after the completion of your program of study (post-completion OPT). Please note, you cannot be your own employer for the purpose of the STEM OPT extension.
If you want to start your own business while you study in the United States, speak to your DSO and visit USCIS’ Entrepreneur Visa Guide.
Practical Training for M Students
M students are only eligible to participate in one type of paid training with an employer, referred to as practical training (PT). Like practical training opportunities for F students, your PT as an M student must directly relate to your program of study. PT allows you to gain training experience in your vocational field.
You may only participate in PT after completing your program. You earn one month of PT eligibility for every four months of program enrollment and must obtain authorization from USCIS before participating in PT.
If you want to learn more about your PT eligibility, speak with your DSO. You may need to apply to USCIS for an extension of stay at the same time you apply for PT authorization, since you are only admitted to the United States for a fixed period of time.
