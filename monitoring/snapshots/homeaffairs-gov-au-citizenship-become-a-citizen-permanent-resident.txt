Become an Australian citizen (by conferral)
Permanent residents including New Zealand Special Category visa (SCV) holders
Becoming a citizen by conferral is a common way to become an Australian citizen. You need to be a permanent resident and meet certain criteria before you can apply.
New Zealand citizens holding an SCV are considered permanent residents for citizenship purposes. This information is for people 59 years and under. If you are 60 years of age or older, see Person 60 years and older. A parent can include a dependent child aged 15 years or younger in their application at no extra cost. The child must be a permanent resident. If a parent is not applying to become an Australian citizen, a child 15 years or younger can apply on their own and must pay the associated fee. See Child 15 years or younger applying on their own. A parent cannot include a child aged 16 or 17 in their application. They must apply on their own. See Persons 16 or 17 years of age. Changes to the permanent residency commencement date apply for certain New Zealand citizens holding an SCV or a New Zealand stream Skilled Independent (subclass 189) visa. Check your eligibility. If you are aged 17 years or under, aged 60 years or over, or have an incapacity or impairment, see the other ways to become an Australian citizen. You may already be an Australian citizen and eligible for Evidence of Australian citizenship if you were born in Australia: and have been ordinarily resident in Australia throughout the first 10 years from your birth to New Zealand citizen parents. Travelling outside Australia
You can apply for citizenship in or outside Australia. We cannot approve your application while you are outside Australia. Let us know if you need to travel outside Australia while your application is being processed. Before you leave, make sure you have a valid passport and Resident Return visa. If you need to travel outside Australia before your ceremony: you must travel on your current passport it should not stop you attending the ceremony it should not delay you in becoming a citizen
You must meet all our eligibility criteria before we will consider your application for citizenship.
When you apply, you must be either:
A permanent resident
You and everyone included in the application must be a permanent resident.
A New Zealand citizen holding a Special Category (subclass 444) visa (SCV)
New Zealand citizens holding an SCV are considered permanent residents for citizenship purposes. You and everyone included in the application must hold a permanent visa or an SCV.
You must also meet these criteria:
Residence requirement
We consider the length of time you have already lived in Australia. In certain cases, we may exempt you from this requirement.
Meet the general residence requirement
When you apply you must: have been living in Australia on a valid visa for 4 years immediately before the day you apply hold a permanent visa or an SCV for the last 12 months immediately before the day you apply and not have been absent from Australia for more than 12 months in total in the past 4 years, including no more than 90 days in total in the 12 months immediately before applying. Children under 16 do not need to meet the general residence requirement but must be a permanent resident. If you are an SCV holder you will continue to be considered a permanent resident for citizenship purposes while outside Australia, provided you held an SCV immediately before your departure. Changes to the permanent residency commencement date apply for certain New Zealand citizens holding an SCV or a New Zealand stream Skilled Independent (subclass 189) visa. See Calculate your permanent residence below.
Calculate your permanent residence
Your residency starts on the date: we granted the permanent visa when you were in Australia or you first entered Australia on a permanent visa. Use our Residence Calculator to see if you meet our requirement.
New Zealand citizens holding a Special Category (subclass 444) visa (SCV)
When calculating permanent residency, the below applies for certain New Zealand citizens holding an SCV. For citizenship purposes: you are eligible to apply for Australian citizenship from 1 July 2023 if we granted your SCV before 1 July 2022 as we consider you to be a permanent resident from 1 July 2022. if you were granted an SCV for the first time on or after 1 July 2022, we consider you to be a permanent resident from the grant date of this visa. The above: applies if you were outside Australia on 1 July 2023 and last held an SCV or Special Purpose Visa (SPV) as an airline crew member or airline positioning crew member immediately before last leaving Australia but does not apply if you were outside Australia because you were removed or deported from Australia under the Migration Act 1958, unless the decision to cancel your visa was revoked. To meet the general residence requirement, when you apply for Australian citizenship, you must also: have been living in Australia on a valid Australian visa for 4 years immediately before the day you apply; and not have been absent from Australia for more than 12 months in the past 4 years. This includes no more than 90 days in the 12 months immediately before applying.
New Zealand citizens holding a New Zealand stream Skilled Independent (subclass 189) visa
When calculating permanent residency, the below applies for New Zealand citizens holding a New Zealand stream Skilled Independent (subclass 189) visa and their New Zealand citizen family members holding a Skilled Independent (subclass 189) visa. For citizenship purposes: you are eligible to apply for Australian citizenship from 1 January 2023 if we granted you this permanent visa between 1 January 2022 and 31 December 2022 as we consider you to be a permanent resident from 1 January 2022. if we granted you this permanent visa on or after 1 January 2023, you are eligible to apply for Australian citizenship from the visa grant date. You do not need to have held this permanent visa for 12 months before you apply if the above applies to you. To meet the general residence requirement, when you apply for Australian citizenship, you must also: have been living in Australia on a valid Australian visa for 4 years immediately before the day you apply; and not have been absent from Australia for more than 12 months in the past 4 years. This includes no more than 90 days in the 12 months immediately before applying.
Exemptions and special circumstances
You do not need to meet our residence requirement if you: are under 16 years were born to a former Australian citizen who lost their citizenship before 4 April 2002 were born in Papua before 16 September 1975 and one of your parents was born in Australia and was an Australian citizen when you were born You will need to give us documents that support your claim when you apply.
Australian Defence Force exemption
You or your family may be exempt from our residence requirement if you are a member of the Australian Defence Force.
Situations when family members are exempt from our residence requirement
We granted you one of these visas on or after 1 July 2007: Labour Agreement (Migrant) visa (class AU) Labour Agreement (Residence) visa (class BV) Employer Nomination (Permanent) visa (class EN) Regional Employer Nomination (Permanent) visa (class RN) AND Your family members were granted the same type of visa on the basis that they were 'members of the family unit' included in your visa application. AND You: were appointed, enlisted or transferred into any of the permanent forces or reserves of the Commonwealth of Australia and have served 90 days in the permanent forces have served a total of 90 paid service days in the navy, army or air force reserve were discharged from defence service as medically unfit for service as a result of that service This also applies if the Australian Defence Force member died while undertaking that service. If you are in this situation, then you and your family members may be exempt from our general residence requirement. You will need to give us proof of your exemption when you apply.
Situations when your family members are not exempt from our residence requirement
We granted you one of these visas on or after 1 July 2007, but members of your family unit subsequently came to Australia on a visa that is not listed: Labour Agreement (Migrant) (class AU) visa Labour Agreement (Residence) (class BV) visa Employer Nomination (Permanent) (class EN) visa Regional Employer Nomination (Permanent) (class RN) visa OR You migrated to Australia for another purpose and subsequently decide to join the permanent forces or the reserves. OR You acquired Australian citizenship automatically (for example, because you were born in Australia) or are an Australian citizen by registration (for example, you were born in Australia but would not be a citizen of another country). If you are in any of these situations, then you and your family members will not be eligible for the Australian Defence Forces exemption. You will need to give us proof that you meet our general residence requirement.
Ministerial discretion
Ministerial discretions that may be applied to assist a person to meet the residence requirement: If you were present in Australia as an unlawful non-citizen and the Minister considers that you were an unlawful non-citizen during that period because of an administrative error, the Minister may treat your presence during that period as if you had been lawful. If you were present in Australia as a temporary resident and the Minister considers that you were not a permanent resident during that period because of an administrative error, the Minister may treat your presence during that period as if you had been a permanent resident. If you were in prison or a psychiatric institution, the Minister may count that period towards the residence requirement if, taking into account the circumstances that resulted in your confinement, the Minister is satisfied that it would be unreasonable not to do so. If you were in Australia as a temporary resident, the Minister may treat that period as one in which you were in Australia as a permanent resident if the Minister is satisfied that you will suffer significant hardship or disadvantage. If you were the spouse or de facto partner, or surviving spouse or de facto partner, of an Australian citizen at the time of your application for citizenship and you had spent time outside Australia while a permanent resident, you were the spouse or de facto partner of the Australian citizen during that period, and you had a close and continuing association with Australia during that period, the Minister may treat that period as one in which you were present in Australia as a permanent resident. If, at the time you applied for citizenship, you held a permanent visa granted to you because you were in an interdependent relationship with an Australian citizen and you were in that interdependent relationship, and you had spent time outside Australia while you held that visa as a permanent resident, and you were in the interdependent relationship with the Australian citizen during that period, and you had a close and continuing association with Australia during that period, the Minister may treat that period as one in which you were present in Australia as a permanent resident. If you wish to have a Ministerial discretion applied to your citizenship application, you must provide supporting evidence. Your eligibility to have a discretion applied cannot be assessed in advance of an application for citizenship.

Character requirement
You must be of 'good character' if you are 18 years or over. Good character is the 'enduring moral qualities of a person'. When we assess good character, we consider whether you are likely to: uphold and obey the laws of Australia meet the other commitments made through the citizenship pledge We may consider: any recorded criminal convictions obligations you may have to a court in Australia or overseas your association with people of concern incidents of reported domestic violence whether you have been honest in your dealings with the Australian community, including providing false or misleading information in relation to a visa or citizenship application
Knowledge of Australia
You must understand what it means to be an Australian citizen. To assess this, most applicants will sit the citizenship test. At the time you have an appointment, you must show that you have: a basic knowledge of the English language an understanding of what it means to become an Australian citizen an adequate knowledge of Australia and the responsibilities and privileges of citizenship an understanding and commitment to Australian values based on freedom, respect and equality If you score 75% or more on the citizenship test, and answer all 5 questions on Australian values correctly, then you meet the knowledge requirement. For more information, see Citizenship test and interview.
Language requirement
You need a basic knowledge of the English language to become an Australian citizen. English is our national language. Communicating in English helps you to live a full life in Australia. If you score 75% or more on the citizenship test, and answer all 5 questions on Australian values correctly, then you meet the language requirement. Some applicants do not have to sit the citizenship test but may have an interview to confirm their identity and show us they understand the nature of their application. For more information, see Citizenship test and interview.
Close and continuing link to Australia
We must be satisfied that you are likely to live or continue to live in Australia, or maintain a close and continuing link with Australia while overseas. Citizenship is a privilege that requires a long-term commitment to Australia. A close and continuing association with family or other social relationships in Australia, including people who are Australian citizens, may not be sufficient to be considered as having a close and continuing link to Australia on its own. When we assess your close and continuing link to Australia, we will consider your living arrangements and migration status, including: if you rent or own property and where this property is located if you have high value items and where these are located, such as a house, property, car or bank account any commitments or ties to Australia, such as your source of income, employment situation, family situation, and schooling arrangements your travel into and out of Australia, your reason for travel, the number of times you travelled into and out of Australia, and how long you were in each place participation in the Australian community, such as being part of community groups, clubs or charities that provides services or opportunities if you have close Australian permanent resident or citizen family members who intend to reside in Australia, such as a spouse, de facto partner or child/ren. For information on the supporting documents we require, see Evidence of close and continuing link to Australia.
We cannot approve your application in certain circumstances
We must not approve your citizenship application if we are not satisfied of your identity. Identity is assessed from birth.
We must not approve your application for Australian citizenship: when proceedings for an offence against an Australian law (including proceedings by way of appeal or review) are pending. when it is less than 2 years since you were released from prison after serving a prison sentence of 12 months or more, or 10 years if you are a repeat offender. you are subject to certain conditions set by an Australian court (such as being released on parole, good behaviour or bail) where action may be taken against you for breach of those conditions. you are in prison or a psychiatric institution by order of the court at the time of decision.
Risk to National security
We must not approve your citizenship application if you are assessed as a risk to the security of Australia.
We must not approve your citizenship application if you ceased to be an Australian citizen in the past 12 months.
Location at time of application approval
If you apply for Australian citizenship on the basis of meeting the requirements for: general eligibility having a permanent or enduring physical or mental incapacity, or being a person aged 60 or over or having a hearing, speech or sight impairment we must not approve your citizenship application if you are outside Australia at the time we decide your application, unless: you satisfied one of the special residence requirements, or the spouse or interdependent partner discretion was applied to assist you to meet the general residence requirement

Good character documents
We need documents to assess if you are of good character.
Overseas penal clearance certificates
Overseas penal clearance certificates, also called police checks in some countries, show if a person has a criminal record outside Australia. We ask for these certificates to help assess if you are of good character. You may need to give us an overseas penal clearance certificate if you have lived or travelled outside Australia since turning 18 years of age.
Permanent Residence Visa holders
Give us an overseas penal clearance certificate if all the following apply: you lived or travelled outside Australia since turning 18 years of age and this was in the last 10 years, or since you got your first permanent resident visa (use whichever is more recent) and you spent a total of 12 months or more outside Australia and you spent 90 days or more in any one country. We may also request a certificate directly from you if required. If New Zealand is one of the countries where you spent time and the above requirements apply: Do not get a New Zealand police certificate yourself. We will request the certificate directly from the New Zealand Ministry of Justice (NZ MOJ). If you have not given us consent in your application, we will contact you to get your consent before making the request.
New Zealand citizen - Special Category Visa (SCV) holders
Give us an overseas penal clearance certificate if, in the last 10 years: you lived or travelled outside Australia since turning 18 years of age and you spent a total of 12 months or more outside Australia and you spent 90 days or more in any one country. We may also request a certificate directly from you if required. If New Zealand is one of the countries where you spent time and the above requirements apply: Do not get a New Zealand police certificate yourself. We will request the certificate directly from the New Zealand Ministry of Justice (NZ MOJ). If you have not given us consent in your application, we will contact you to get your consent before making the request.
How to get an overseas penal clearance certificate
You can get an overseas penal clearance certificate from the relevant government or law enforcement authority in the country where you lived. Guidance on how to apply for an overseas penal clearance certificate or police check in specific countries is available at Offices outside Australia > Police checks. For more information, including what to do if you can’t give us an overseas penal clearance certificate, see Character requirements for Australian Citizenship.

Most people aged between 18 and 59 years will have an appointment to sit the citizenship test. We will send you an appointment letter with the details. The time from when you apply to your appointment date varies, but can take several months. Some applicants do not have to sit the citizenship test but may have an interview with us. Most applicants for citizenship by conferral must attend a citizenship ceremony to make the pledge of commitment to become an Australian citizen. You will receive your citizenship certificate after you make the pledge of commitment at your ceremony. If you need to attend a ceremony, you will be sent an invitation letter, generally about four weeks before the ceremony. See Citizenship ceremony. Wait times for citizenship ceremonies can vary. Check that your name and date of birth are correct on the certificate. If they are wrong, contact us as soon as possible.

If we made an error with your citizenship certificate
If you believe we made an error on your certificate, contact us as soon as possible. For example, if we printed your name or date of birth incorrectly. If we made the error, you may not need to apply to change your citizenship certificate if you let us know within six weeks of receiving your certificate. You may need to apply to change your evidence of Australian citizenship. This applies if you: let us know of the error after the six weeks’ time frame, or provided incorrect details when applying which resulted in the error. If we made the error, we can refund the evidence application fee. We cannot refund this fee if you provided incorrect details when applying which resulted in the error. For more information on how to request a refund, see getting a refund.
Care of an Australian citizenship certificate
An Australian citizenship certificate is an important legal document that you must look after. It is an offence under section 39 of the Australian Citizenship Act 2007 to alter this document, or to allow it to be altered by another person. You must not write on or change any details on the certificate. Some Australian Government departments and agencies, as well as private organisations may refuse to accept laminated or damaged certificates.
Getting an Australian passport
You cannot apply for an Australian passport until you have received evidence of your citizenship from us. For information about applying for an Australian passport, including how long it takes, visit the Australian passport office website.
