You can be Belgian by law or voluntarily. You can also lose your Belgian nationality. Consult the "Nationality" section on the website of the Federal Public Service Foreign Affairs.
Below is an overview of the most common cases:
1. Granting of Belgian nationality before the child's 5th birthday
2. Acquiring Belgian nationality after the age of 18
3. Acquiring Belgian nationality through marriage
4. Loss of Belgian nationality
5. Reacquiring Belgian nationality
6. Possessing Belgian citizenship
7. Possessing multiple nationalities
1. Granting of Belgian nationality before the child's 5th birthday
If you are Belgian and born outside Belgium, and your child was also born outside Belgium, your child is not automatically Belgian. Provided that the parentage is validly established under Belgian law, the Belgian parent must sign a declaration of granting Belgian nationality before the child's 5th birthday.
2. Acquiring Belgian nationality after the age of 18
Since January 1, 2013, an adult can become Belgian after a voluntary personal application, either through a declaration of nationality or through naturalization. These procedures are only open to persons whose primary residence is in Belgium, i.e., those registered in the population register, the aliens register, or the waiting list of a Belgian municipality.
The Consulate General is not authorized in this regard. Please contact the Belgian municipality where you are registered.
3. Acquiring Belgian nationality through marriage
Marriage does not automatically confer the right to Belgian nationality. Certain conditions must be met. One of these conditions is that the primary residence must be in Belgium.
The Consulate General is not authorized in this regard. Please contact the Belgian municipality where you are registered.
4. Loss of Belgian nationality
In some cases, you may lose your Belgian nationality.
4.1. If you do not obtain a retention declaration before your 28th birthday,
If you simultaneously meet the following 5 conditions, you will lose your Belgian nationality on your 28th birthday:
1. You were born abroad (outside Belgium) after January 1, 1967, and
2. You have one or more foreign nationalities, regardless of how you acquired these nationalities or whether they were granted, and
3. You never had your primary residence in Belgium between your 18th and 28th birthdays, and
4. You cannot claim to have held a position abroad in the service of the Belgian government or within a company/association under Belgian law, and
5. You did not apply for and receive a Belgian passport or identity card between your 18th and 28th birthdays.
If you wish to retain your Belgian nationality, you must apply for and receive a passport or identity card between your 18th and 28th birthdays. This provision applies only to persons who were not yet 28 years old on July 12, 2018. Consult the procedure you must follow to apply for a passport or to apply for an identity card.
Alternatively, you can sign a declaration of retention of Belgian nationality at the consulate where you are registered before your 28th birthday.
4.2. Voluntary Acquisition of a Foreign Nationality from the Age of 18
1. If you voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality before June 9, 2007, you have lost your Belgian nationality.
2. If you voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality between June 9, 2007, and April 28, 2008, you have not lost your Belgian nationality, unless the voluntary nationality you acquired is that of one of the following countries: Denmark, Austria, Spain, France, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, Norway, the Netherlands, or the United Kingdom. Please note: Section 4.1 (failure to retain a nationality declaration before your 28th birthday) remains applicable.
3. If you voluntarily acquire a foreign nationality after April 28, 2008, you will no longer lose your Belgian nationality, regardless of the nationality acquired. Please note: Section 4.1 (failure to retain a nationality declaration before your 28th birthday) remains applicable.
4.3. By renunciation
An adult Belgian who has another nationality can sign a renunciation of Belgian nationality at the municipality where they are registered if they live in Belgium, or at the consulate where they are registered if they live abroad.
5. Reacquisition of Belgian nationality
Someone who has lost their Belgian nationality in the past can regain it under certain conditions.
Since July 12, 2018, it has been possible to apply for the reacquisition of Belgian nationality.
From a Belgian consular post abroad, but only if the loss of Belgian nationality is due to the inability to sign a declaration of retention of Belgian nationality before your 28th birthday, or if you did not apply for and receive a passport or identity card between your 18th and 28th birthdays. The public prosecutor decides whether or not to regain Belgian nationality after assessing the circumstances in which the applicant lost Belgian nationality and the reasons for wanting to regain it.
If you wish to submit an application for the recovery of Belgian nationality, please contact the Nationality Department by email at hongkong@diplobel.fed.be.
6. Possession of Belgian Nationality
Since July 12, 2018, it has been possible to sign a declaration of possession of Belgian nationality at a Belgian consular post abroad if Belgian nationality, wrongly granted, was revoked after the person in question had enjoyed Belgian nationality for at least 10 years. This declaration must be signed within one year of the final decision that Belgian nationality is disputed. The public prosecutor will grant or deny Belgian nationality based on the declaration of nationality in the case of possession of Belgian nationality.
For more information about the declaration of possession of Belgian nationality, please contact the Nationality Department by email at hongkong@diplobel.fed.be
7. Possession of multiple nationalities
Currently, you no longer lose your Belgian nationality if you voluntarily acquire another nationality.
If you are Belgian and automatically acquire another nationality (this is without any action on your part, for example, by birth because your parents have a foreign nationality), you do not lose your Belgian nationality and can be a dual national or hold multiple nationalities.
If you hold a foreign nationality and acquire Belgian nationality, the Belgian authorities will not ask you to renounce your original nationality. However, it is advisable to ask your national government whether acquiring Belgian nationality could entail the loss of your original nationality under your country's law. You will always be considered Belgian by the Belgian government.
